Read: two structurally different attacks — full merchant redirect, and a same-merchant price inflation — both bypass the naive agent and both get caught by the gate. The gate isn't pattern-matching the injection text; it's comparing the final mandate against the trusted catalog record. That's why it holds up against an attack shape it wasn't specifically tuned for.
Honest limitation: the injection marker scan (pattern-based) failed on both attacks — it's a weak layer on its own, beatable by an attacker avoiding known trigger phrases. The evidence-decision consistency check is what actually holds; frame the gate as blast-radius reduction, not a guarantee.

TRACK D — FEEDBACK-LOOP POISONING

Attacking the defense's own retraining pipeline

Instead of evading the fraud model, this attacks the label stream it retrains on — silent fraud auto-labeled "legit" because no chargeback was filed. Real IEEE-CIS data, 51 features.

Blind-spot recall — inside the attacker's operating region

Overall AUC barely moves across all three scenarios (0.936 → 0.933 → 0.936) — the damage is only visible when you measure recall inside the attacker's region. That's the entire point of this attack.

Documented negative result: the textbook defense — leave-cluster-out validation against a trusted holdout — was built and tested first, and caught zero poison. A targeted backdoor creates a blind spot in a narrow region; if the holdout doesn't cover that region, the screen stays silent. This generalizes: validation-based poison detection only sees poison in regions the validation set covers. That finding is why disagreement-screening (above) exists instead.
